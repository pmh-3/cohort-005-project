# In-app notification patterns: launches and mentions

Research for [Research notification patterns for course launches and mentions](https://github.com/pmh-3/cohort-005-project/issues/9), informing [Specify in-app notifications for Students and Instructors](https://github.com/pmh-3/cohort-005-project/issues/1).

**Checked:** 2026-11-15. **Status:** evidence, not Cadence requirements or product decisions.

## Scope and method

Compared three platforms: **Canvas LMS** (learning), **Discourse** (community), and **Slack** (community/workplace). Read the owning vendors' documentation, not search snippets. Canvas pages were retrieved successfully with `curl` after the web fetcher failed; Discourse's official documentation posts were read through their `/raw/` endpoints, using the authored first post rather than community replies. Sources and relevant sections are listed below.

“Documented” means stated by the cited source, not independently tested in a live account. **Unknown** means the reviewed evidence does not establish the behavior, not that the feature is absent. Announcement topics/posts are explicit **analogues**, not proof of an automatic new-course event. Email, browser banners/push, and mobile push are outside Cadence's scope; channel-specific documentation is not treated as an in-app guarantee. No engagement-lift evidence was established or is claimed.

## Comparison

### Canvas LMS

- **Recipients and event semantics — documented:** Creating a course/group announcement is a notification trigger **and** a Course Activity Stream event. The notification reference limits recipients generally to active, participating enrollments; concluded/restricted enrollments do not receive notifications. [C1] The announcement authoring guide supports course-wide or selected-section audiences. It says the course must be published for students to receive announcement notifications; announcements created before publication and imported announcements do not send new-announcement notifications. Scheduled announcements use their “Available From” time and recipients' settings. These are documented announcement-notification rules; the guide does not separately establish every delivery channel's behavior. **Unknown:** an automatic catalog-wide course-publication alert, republishing behavior, or backfill for later enrollees. [C2]
- **Mentions and access — documented:** Students can select another course member with `@` in a discussion reply; the ability requires a course-role permission and is unavailable in anonymous discussions. Delivery depends on the recipient's “Discussions — New Mention” setting. [C3] Crucially, the reference marks a new discussion/announcement mention as a notification-settings event but **not** a Course Activity Stream event. **Unknown:** a dedicated in-app mention inbox from these sources; selection/suppression rules for hidden discussions, section-restricted content, and subsequently revoked access. Course membership alone is not evidence that every source is readable. [C1, C3]
- **In-app surface and unread behavior — documented:** The Course Activity Stream includes announcements, discussions, assignments, and conversations. New activity has a blue dot; items can be expanded, opened, or removed, and remain for four weeks. **Unknown:** whether opening the stream or expanding/opening an item clears its dot, and whether removal changes another unread count. This is a course activity surface, not evidence of a universal notification bell. [C4]
- **Controls/defaults — documented, channel boundary matters:** Contact-method notification defaults include “Announcement: Notify immediately,” “New Mention: Notify immediately,” and “Announcement Created By You: Notifications off.” They are **not in-app opt-out defaults**: the same reference explicitly says notification settings do not affect the Course Activity Stream and its updates cannot be disabled, although users can choose not to use that stream where applicable. Do not copy contact-method settings into an assumed bell policy. [C1]

### Discourse

- **Recipients and announcement analogue — documented:** The official announcement recipe creates a category with restricted posting and configures `default categories watching first post`; admins can apply the change to existing users as well as future users. New topics trigger notifications; replies do not merely because the category is watched for first posts. The recipe describes onsite notifications for active users and email according to preferences for inactive users; it does **not** establish whether an inactive user's onsite record is omitted or retained. This is configured announcement-topic creation, not automatic course publication. [D1] “Watching First Post” applies to categories/tags, then leaves the topic at Normal unless another tracking rule applies. [D2]
- **Mentions and access — documented:** `@username` mentions notify the named user. [D3] Private-category notification setup requires group access; its guide explicitly says only members of the permitted group receive those category notifications, even when a default watch preference is applied broadly. **Unknown:** mention-specific handling for a named user lacking source access, and cleanup of an old notification after access is removed. The category-subscription rule is not proof of those mention/lifecycle rules. [D4]
- **In-app surface and unread behavior — documented:** Other notifications, including mentions/replies/likes, use a blue avatar counter; personal messages have a green envelope with higher indicator priority. Opening the avatar removes the indicator and shows the notification menu. Unread entries remain highlighted at the top; filters separate types, and “Dismiss” marks the current tab's notifications read. **Inference:** indicator acknowledgement and item-read state should be considered separately; the guide describes both rather than equating opening the menu with reading every item. [D5]
- **Controls/defaults — documented:** Fresh-site default category/tag lists are empty, leaving them at **Normal** (mentions and direct replies), not automatically watching all new content. Users can override category/tag/topic preferences. Watching adds every reply; Tracking adds counts while retaining mention/reply notifications; Muted is documented as suppressing all notifications for that topic. Admins can configure defaults and apply changes historically. **Unknown:** the exact preference state of any particular deployed community and precedence among every conflicting category/tag/topic rule. [D2]

### Slack

- **Recipients and announcement analogue — documented:** Activity's Channels filter includes messages from channels configured for **All new posts**. Users can choose All new posts or Just mentions per channel/group DM, and separately choose whether these channel messages appear in Activity. Thus an ordinary announcement post can be surfaced through a subscribed channel; these sources do not establish an automatic course event or a universally subscribed announcement audience. [S1, S2, S3]
- **Mentions and access — documented, with a source conflict:** The dedicated mentions guide says joined-channel members are notified; a nonmember mentioned in a **public** channel receives an Activity-feed notification; a nonmember of a **private** channel is not notified and cannot see the message until invited. Someone outside a DM is not notified. [S4] However, Slack's general actions guide says people outside a channel are **not notified by default**, offering add/notify/do-nothing choices. [S5] These statements conflict for public-channel nonmembers. The dedicated guide explicitly names Activity, but no account test reconciled the discrepancy: **public-nonmember delivery is uncertain**, not a settled precedent. Both agree that a mention is not sufficient access to a private conversation. [S4, S5]
- **In-app surface and unread behavior — documented:** Activity is an inbox-like feed with an unread-notification badge, type filters, and an Unreads filter. Users can mark entries read individually/in bulk. Replying marks an entry read; clearing hides it in the main feed, preserves it under Cleared notifications, and marks it read. [S1] **Unknown:** whether merely opening Activity marks every visible entry read; the cited instructions do not establish that.
- **Controls/defaults — documented, not a universal mute:** Slack distinguishes banner delivery preferences from “What to show in Activity.” The latter always includes mentions, reactions, and thread replies while allowing selection of other categories; the reviewed page does not specify initial checkbox defaults for all optional categories. [S2] Conversation-level All new posts/Just mentions and mute controls exist. [S3] Muting does **not** erase every in-app signal: mention/DM numbered badges still appear beside muted conversations, and Threads can become bold for replies. **Unknown:** a single opt-out that suppresses every mention surface; do not infer it from “mute.” [S6]

## Implications and trade-offs — analysis, not decisions

1. **Launch ≠ announcement creation.** Canvas separates course publication prerequisites from announcement creation; Discourse's analogue is a new topic, not subsequent replies. [C2, D1] Cadence could key relevance to availability, publication, or an explicit announcement, but these have different repeat-event and audience implications. The evidence does not choose one.
2. **Targeting ≠ access ≠ subscription.** Canvas offers course/section targeting, Discourse combines visibility with watch settings, and Slack distinguishes private access from public-channel membership. [C2, D4, S4] A broadly relevant launch and a directly addressed mention need not share a recipient rule. Permission checks at event time versus viewing time remain a Cadence question.
3. **A bell count needs a meaning.** Canvas's activity dot, Discourse's acknowledged indicator plus highlighted unread entries, and Slack's explicit read/clear operations are different models. [C4, D5, S1] A simple popover has less interaction complexity; an inbox makes triage and revisiting easier but introduces state choices. This is a design trade-off, not measured evidence that either improves engagement.
4. **“Off” needs a channel and scope.** Canvas contact preferences leave the activity stream intact; Slack mute leaves mention badges; Discourse exposes topic/category defaults and user overrides. [C1, S6, D2] Global, event-type, and course-level controls offer different simplicity/relevance trade-offs. Neither mandatory mentions nor opt-out launches follow automatically from these examples.

## Evidence gaps and limits

- **Learning-platform fallback:** [Moodle Notifications](https://docs.moodle.org/en/Notifications) and [Announcements](https://docs.moodle.org/en/Announcements) could not be read: HTTP 403 challenge; a versioned Notifications URL also returned 403. No Moodle behavior is used as evidence. Canvas supplied usable first-party learning-platform coverage.
- None of the reviewed sources establishes the full semantics of a Cadence-like course becoming newly available: first publication versus republishing, availability by country/cohort, self-notification, duplicate suppression, enrollment after launch, or changes during fan-out. These remain unknown, not requirements inferred from announcement analogues.
- Mention-after-edit, deletion, inaccessible-source previews, access revocation, and historical notification redaction are not established across the compared products. Canvas mention in-app delivery is especially underdetermined: its documented activity stream excludes that event. [C1]
- No live tenant/account tests or API/source-code validation were performed. Vendor docs can change; Slack's contradictory public-nonmember instructions warrant a focused test or vendor clarification if that distinction matters. [S4, S5] Default claims above refer only to the explicitly documented setting/channel, not all installations.

## Questions for the human — intentionally unanswered

- For [Define new-course triggers and recipients](https://github.com/pmh-3/cohort-005-project/issues/3): What makes a course “new” to a Student or Instructor: first publication, newly granted availability, or an explicit announcement? Who should find that actionable, including existing enrollees, potential enrollees, the author, and people gaining access later? Should republishing announce again?
- For [Define mention eligibility and prerequisites](https://github.com/pmh-3/cohort-005-project/issues/4): Which existing interaction can host mentions, if any? Must a recipient already be able to read it? If access later disappears, what may the notification reveal or link to? This research does not authorize building a mentioning interaction.
- For [Define notification controls and defaults](https://github.com/pmh-3/cohort-005-project/issues/5): Does opting out suppress creation, the badge, or just a category in the feed? Are launch and mention choices independent, and is their scope global or per course? Does a later preference change affect old items?
- For [Choose the bell and reading experience](https://github.com/pmh-3/cohort-005-project/issues/6): Does the badge mean unseen or unread? Does opening a menu, opening a source, or explicit acknowledgement mark an item read? Is clearing distinct from reading, and must read items remain revisitable?
